<InterpretSection title="About extreme heat season">

The extreme heat season is the portion of the year when extreme heat days
typically occur. An extreme heat day is when the maximum temperature exceeds a
defined threshold that poses a significant risk to human health, ecosystems, and
infrastructure.

The extreme heat season is generally expected to expand due to global warming,
with extreme heat occurring both earlier and later in the year than in the
historical period[@intergovernmentalpanelonclimatechangeipccClimate2023; @habeebRising2015].
This change in timing will have operational impacts on the
energy sector, including by altering the annual cycle of heating and cooling
demand.

</InterpretSection>

<InterpretSection title="How to interpret this figure">

The heat map shows how the annual timing and frequency of extreme heat is
projected to change as global warming increases, based on the location and
threshold selected. Each row represents a day of the year from 1 (January 1) to
365 (December 31), excluding leap days. Each column represents a different
global warming level (GWL).

Looking from left to right, the axis shows an increasing amount of global
warming (today’s conditions → 1.5°C warming → 2°C warming, etc.). The specific
year that a given GWL will be reached depends on future emissions and societal
decisions.

<Callout kind="example" label="Example interpretation">

The days when extreme heat is most likely might shift from occurring mainly in
July-August today (GWL 0.8°C) to beginning in June and extending into September
(GWL 2.0°C), affecting when peak cooling capacity is needed and overlapping more
with wildfire season.

</Callout>
