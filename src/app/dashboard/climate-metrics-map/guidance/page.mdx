# Climate Metrics Map: Guidance & Methods

## About the Climate Metrics Map

Showing the absolute and change in extreme weather across heat, precipitation,
and fire weather over the potential futures in California allows individuals,
planners, researchers, and interested parties to examine the general shape of
climate projections.

## Projections Type

- **Absolute:** show the average expected value for the chosen metric at the
  selected Global Warming Level (GWL).
- **Delta:** show the change between a 0.8 °C world (approximately 1987-2016)
  and the selected GWL.

## Global Warming Level

Show what different parts of California will look like when the world, as a
whole, has increased average temperature compared to pre-industrial by the
chosen amount.

For additional information, see
[Understanding Climate Futures Through the Lens of Global Warming Levels](https://analytics.cal-adapt.org/scientific-guidance/global-warming-levels.html).

Use the dropdown menu to select a global warming level (e.g., 1.5 °C, 2.0 °C).
This will adjust the data overlays to reflect projected changes under the
selected warming level.

## Metric

Choose a climate metric to display on the map (e.g., extreme temperature,
extreme precipitation, fire weather index). Each metric provides a unique
perspective on how climate change impacts various regions. A plain language
description of each metric can be found below.

## Interactive Map Features

- **Pan and Zoom:** Click and drag to move the map, and use the scroll wheel
  or zoom buttons to focus on specific areas.
- **Region Highlighting:** Click on a region to view localized climate data
  and projections.
- **Legend:** The color scale on the map legend indicates the range of values
  for the selected metric. All of these maps may be used for many things. Our
  "Takeaway" is not the limit of useful information but a common use for this
  map.

## Methods

The sections above describe how to use the map. The definitions below
describe what each metric means, exactly how it was calculated, and a common
takeaway for interpreting it.

### Fire Weather

<Specs>
<Spec label="Metric">

Change in median annual number of days with Fosberg Fire Weather Index (FFWI)
value > 50

</Spec>
<Spec label="Description">

How many more days a year there is weather that is conducive to wildfires.
The FFWI is based on changes in temperature, wind, and humidity. A FFWI value
of 50 or greater indicates significant fire weather conditions.

</Spec>
<Spec label="Technical definition">

Number of FFWI days above a value of 50 in relation to the historical
baseline of 0.8 °C GWL

</Spec>
<Spec label="Takeaway">

Shows how often the weather is conducive for fires in this area.

</Spec>
</Specs>

### Extreme Heat

<Specs>
<Spec label="Metric">

Mean annual change in extreme heat days

</Spec>
<Spec label="Description">

How many more extremely hot days there are in a year. Extremely hot is
defined as above the peak temperature that has occurred in the past,
historically about 3 to 4 of the hottest days of the year.

</Spec>
<Spec label="Technical definition">

Number of maximum air temperature days exceeding the 99th percentile in
relation to the historical baseline of 0.8 °C GWL

</Spec>
<Spec label="Takeaway">

Shows how many days during the year are expected to be very hot compared to
the past.

</Spec>
</Specs>

### Extreme Precipitation

<Specs>
<Spec label="Metric">

Absolute change in 99th percentile 1-day accumulated precipitation (mm)

</Spec>
<Spec label="Description">

How much more rain falls on really heavy rain days.

</Spec>
<Spec label="Technical definition">

Change in 99th percentile precipitation, excluding snowfall, in relation to
the historical baseline of 0.8 °C GWL

</Spec>
<Spec label="Takeaway">

Shows how much more precipitation will fall on really heavy precipitation
days. Much more of California's precipitation will be coming in the form of
extreme events, i.e., extreme, problematic events will be more frequent. A
change in those types of extreme events strains our resources and
infrastructure.

</Spec>
</Specs>
