# Heat Waves: Data Methods

The steps below describe how the values shown in the tool were
calculated, from raw climate model output to the final data product.

## Input data

<Specs title="Climate model simulations">
<Spec label="Description">

Ensemble of 4 bias-adjusted climate model simulations, dynamically downscaled
using the WRF model for the state of California: `EC-Earth3-r1i1p1f1`,
`MPI-ESM1-2-HR-r3i1p1f1`, `TaiESM1-r1i1p1f1`, `MIROC6-r1i1p1f1`.

</Spec>
<Spec label="Variables">

Maximum daily air temperature

</Spec>
<Spec label="Spatial resolution">

3 kilometers

</Spec>
<Spec label="Temporal resolution">

Daily

</Spec>
<Spec label="Time period">

1980-2100: the modeled historical period (1980-2014), followed by the SSP3-7.0 scenario (2015-2100)

</Spec>

## Calculation steps

<Step n={1} totalSteps={7} title="Data retrieval and preprocessing">

Using Cal-Adapt's open source Python library
[`climakitae`](https://github.com/cal-adapt/climakitae), input data was
retrieved. Climate model simulations were retrieved for historical and future
global warming levels (GWL): 0.8, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0 °C. This retrieval
process pulls the 30 year time window around the year (+/- 15 years) in which
the global warming level is reached. A 30 year window is used to look at typical
conditions over a long period centered on the global warming level.

Inland water was masked from the data to discard artificially high values over
water that are not representative of the variable.

</Step>

<Step n={2} totalSteps={7} title="Percentile-based threshold">

For percentile-based thresholds, a per-gridcell historical temperature threshold
was calculated using the 0.8 GWL for each simulation. This value represents an
extreme heat threshold unique to the historical conditions at each location.

</Step>

<Step n={3} totalSteps={7} title="Heat waves in each year">

This step differs between the two metrics. Each was calculated for each
maximum temperature threshold (e.g. 95 °F, 100 °F, 95th percentile), simulation,
and GWL. Absolute thresholds range from 85 to 115 °F and percentile-based
thresholds from the 90th to the 99th percentile.

#### Heat wave frequency

For each threshold and streak duration (from 3 to 14 days) pair, the **number of
streaks of consecutive days exceeding the threshold in each year** was counted.
Streaks were non-overlapping (any single day can only belong to one streak, if
any) but may be consecutive. For example, if the 3-day duration is selected, a
6-day streak that exceeds the temperature threshold would be counted as two
3-day streaks.

<Callout title="Plain language example">

If a specific location has a historical 95th percentile maximum temperature of
100 °F, this represents an "extreme heat" day in the historical period. Step 3
asks: under \+1.5 °C of global warming, how many 3-day streaks in each year in
that gridcell will exceed 100 °F? When asking this question using a fixed
temperature threshold such as 95 °F, it changes to: how many 3-day periods with
maximum temperatures greater than 95 °F will occur in a typical year?

</Callout>

#### Heat wave length

For each threshold, the **average length of streaks of consecutive days
exceeding the threshold per year** was calculated. Only streaks with a length of
at least 3 days are included in this average. A streak that runs from one year
into the next is split, and each part is counted in its own year.

<Callout title="Plain language example">

If a specific location has a historical 95th percentile maximum temperature of
100 °F, this represents an "extreme heat" day in the historical period. Step 3
asks: under \+1.5 °C of global warming, what is the average length of a period of
days that all exceed 100 °F in that gridcell? When asking this question using a
fixed temperature threshold such as 95 °F, it changes to: what is the average
length of a period of days that all exceed 95 °F in any given year?

</Callout>

</Step>

<Step n={4} totalSteps={7} title="Median and variability over GWL time window">

The following metrics were calculated for each simulation across the 30-year
period corresponding to each GWL: median, 10th percentile, 90th percentile. This
yields a single value per metric per simulation.

<Callout title="Why compute the 10th and 90th percentile?">

The 10th and 90th percentile values represent the number or length of heat waves
in a particularly cold year and particularly hot year, respectively. Together,
these provide an estimate of the range of year-to-year variability under each
warming level. These 10th and 90th percentiles specifically represent conditions
that would be expected to occur once every 10 years on average.

</Callout>

</Step>

<Step n={5} totalSteps={7} title="Change signal">

For each GWL above the reference period (1.5 °C and higher) and for each metric
(median, 10th percentile, 90th percentile), the historical baseline value was
subtracted from the future GWL value. This difference represents the projected
change in heat wave frequency or length relative to the historical period.

<Callout title="Plain language example">

If a grid cell experiences six 3-day heat waves above 100 °F in the historical
period, how many **more** 3-day heat waves will that same grid cell experience
under \+1.5 °C (and 2.0 and so on) of global warming? Likewise, if the average
length of a heat wave above 100 °F is 5 days in the historical period, by how
many days will the average heat wave length increase?

</Callout>

</Step>

<Step n={6} totalSteps={7} title="Spatial aggregation">

Next, we aggregated the gridded data by spatial unit (e.g. county, city, etc) by
taking a **median** of all the grid cells within the boundary. A median is used
to capture the typical number or length of heat waves within the area of interest
without introducing bias from any anomalous grid cells.

<Callout title="Grid cells at the edge">

All grid cells within or touching the spatial boundary were included (even if not
fully contained within the boundary) to avoid discarding data representative of
conditions at the edge. Additionally, small spatial units such as census tracts
may not fully contain any single grid cell, so an all-touched approach is
generally used across Cal Adapt for consistency across spatial scales.

</Callout>

</Step>

<Step n={7} totalSteps={7} title="Simulation aggregation">

Finally, the data was aggregated across simulations using a mean. The final data
product is a table for each county containing the following variables:

#### Absolute variables

- Annual average frequency of heat waves, with its 10th and 90th percentile (for each GWL, threshold, and duration)
- Annual average length of heat wave, with its 10th and 90th percentile (for each GWL and threshold)

#### Relative change variables

- Change from reference period: annual average frequency of heat waves, with its 10th and 90th percentile (for each GWL, threshold, and duration)
- Change from reference period: annual average length of heat wave, with its 10th and 90th percentile (for each GWL and threshold)

</Step>
