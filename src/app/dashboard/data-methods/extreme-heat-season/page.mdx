# Extreme Heat Season: Data Methods

The steps below describe how the values shown in the tool were
calculated, from raw climate model output to the final data product.

## Input data

<Specs title="Climate model simulations">
<Spec label="Description">

Ensemble of 4 bias-adjusted climate model simulations, dynamically downscaled
using the WRF model for the state of California: `EC-Earth3-r1i1p1f1`,
`MPI-ESM1-2-HR-r3i1p1f1`, `TaiESM1-r1i1p1f1`, `MIROC6-r1i1p1f1`.

</Spec>
<Spec label="Variables">

Maximum daily air temperature

</Spec>
<Spec label="Spatial resolution">

3 kilometers

</Spec>
<Spec label="Temporal resolution">

Daily

</Spec>
<Spec label="Time period">

1980-2100: the modeled historical period (1980-2014), followed by the SSP3-7.0 scenario (2015-2100)

</Spec>
<Spec label="Download">

Browse in the <StacBrowserLink collection="wrf-ucla">Cal-Adapt data catalog</StacBrowserLink> (STAC), or download directly from <S3FolderLink folder="wrf/ucla/">cloud storage</S3FolderLink> (AWS).

</Spec>
</Specs>

## Output data

<Specs title="Regional tables">
<Spec label="Description">

The percent of years in which each calendar day is an extreme heat day, for each threshold and global warming level, averaged across simulations

</Spec>
<Spec label="Shown in">

[Extreme Heat Tool](/dashboard/extreme-heat-days), [Extreme Heat Climate Story](/dashboard/climate-stories/extreme-heat)

</Spec>
<Spec label="Format">

One CSV table per region

</Spec>
<Spec label="Download">

Browse in the <StacBrowserLink collection="ehs-metrics-mm-boundary-csv">Cal-Adapt data catalog</StacBrowserLink> (STAC), or download directly from <S3FolderLink folder="wrf/extreme-heat-season/multimodel_per_boundary/">cloud storage</S3FolderLink> (AWS).

</Spec>
</Specs>

## Calculation steps

<Step n={1} totalSteps={5} title="Data retrieval and preprocessing">

Using Cal-Adapt's open source Python library
[`climakitae`](https://github.com/cal-adapt/climakitae), input data was
retrieved. Climate model simulations were retrieved for historical and future
global warming levels (GWL): 0.8, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0 °C. This retrieval
process pulls the 30 year time window around the year (+/- 15 years) in which
the global warming level is reached. A 30 year window is used to look at typical
conditions over a long period centered on the global warming level.

Inland water was masked from the data to discard artificially high values over
water that are not representative of the variable. Leap days were removed so that every year has 365
days.

</Step>

<Step n={2} totalSteps={5} title="Percentile-based threshold">

For percentile-based thresholds, a per-gridcell historical temperature threshold
was calculated using the 0.8 GWL for each simulation. This value represents an
extreme heat threshold unique to the historical conditions at each location.

</Step>

<Step n={3} totalSteps={5} title="Exceedance frequency by calendar day">

For each fixed maximum temperature threshold, or historically calculated
percentile threshold, the **number of years in which each calendar day exceeds
the threshold** was counted across the 30-year window for each GWL, for each day
of the year from 1 (January 1) to 365 (December 31). This count is expressed as
a percent of the 30 years. Absolute thresholds range from 65 to 135 °F and
percentile-based thresholds from the 90th to the 99th percentile.

<Callout title="Plain language example">

If a specific location has a historical 95th percentile maximum temperature of
100 °F, this represents an "extreme heat" day in the historical period. Step 3
asks: under \+1.5 °C of global warming, during how many years of the 30-year
warming level window does day 200 exceed 100 °F? When asking this question using
a fixed temperature threshold, it changes to: during how many years of the
30-year warming level window does day 150 exceed 90 °F?

</Callout>

</Step>

<Step n={4} totalSteps={5} title="Spatial aggregation">

Next, we aggregated the gridded data by spatial unit (e.g. county, city, etc) by
taking a **median** of all the grid cells within the boundary. A median is used
to capture the typical frequency of extreme heat days within the area of interest
without introducing bias from any anomalous grid cells.

<Callout title="Grid cells at the edge">

All grid cells within or touching the spatial boundary were included (even if not
fully contained within the boundary) to avoid discarding data representative of
conditions at the edge. Additionally, small spatial units such as census tracts
may not fully contain any single grid cell, so an all-touched approach is
generally used across Cal Adapt for consistency across spatial scales.

</Callout>

</Step>

<Step n={5} totalSteps={5} title="Simulation aggregation">

Finally, the data was aggregated across simulations using a mean. The final data
product is a table for each county containing the following variable:

- Percent of years in which the day is an extreme heat day (for each GWL, threshold, and day of year)

</Step>
