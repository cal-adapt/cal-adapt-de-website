# Heating & Cooling Degree Days: Guidance & Methods

## What are cooling degree days and heating degree days?

Cooling degree days (CDDs) measure how much and for how long outdoor temperatures exceed a specific threshold, indicating the need for indoor cooling. The threshold of 65 °F is used to approximate the point at which cooling becomes necessary.

Heating degree days (HDDs) quantify how much and for how long outdoor temperatures fall below a specific threshold, reflecting demand for indoor heating. The threshold of 65 °F is used to approximate the point at which heating becomes necessary.

On a given day, the CDD/HDD value is a measurement of how many degrees the daily average temperature falls above/below the 65 °F threshold. The annual CDD/HDD value is a sum of the daily CDD/HDD values.

## How are cooling degree days and heating degree days used in energy sector planning?

Cooling degree days are used widely across the energy sector to help translate outdoor temperature into estimated energy demand[@auffhammerMeasuring2014; @thomRational1954]. The use of air conditioning on hot days is a major driver of peak electricity usage across the state, and CDDs provide a simple metric to estimate how this demand may grow with temperature increases under global warming.

As more indoor heating moves from gas to electric, heating degree days play an increasingly important role in estimating how changes in winter temperatures will impact electricity demand.

Within California, the California Energy Commission and major utilities use CDDs and HDDs computed from climate projections as inputs to their electricity demand forecasts[@cec-2025-demand-side-modeling].

## Limitations

Cooling and heating degree days are a useful but highly simplified metric for estimating how temperature will translate into electricity demand. In reality, the temperature thresholds that trigger the use of indoor heating or cooling vary depending on region, building type, season, and individual behavior. The 65 °F threshold is a recognized standard that is useful for broad comparisons, but more in-depth electricity demand forecasting involves empirically measuring the relationship between temperature and electricity usage.

Additionally, cooling and heating degree days are calculated from daily average temperature, and do not reflect how changes in the range of hourly temperatures throughout the day may impact the use of indoor heating or cooling.

## What is the SSP3-7.0 scenario?

The latest generation of climate simulations use a standardized set of emissions scenarios that describe a range of possible futures through the end of the 21st century. Each of these scenarios, which are known as Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs), describes a plausible future of global technological and economic development and estimates the annual global greenhouse gas emissions that would be produced. The scenarios range from a "Fossil-fueled Development" scenario where no action is taken to reduce global greenhouse gas emissions (SSP5-8.5) to an ambitious sustainable pathway that keeps global warming under 2 °C (SSP1-2.6). The SSP3-7.0 scenario is a "medium high" emissions scenario that is commonly used for impact assessments and adaptation planning, as it captures a balance between highly optimistic and "worst case" scenarios.

## Methods

### Input data

<Specs>
<Spec label="Variable">

Average daily air temperature

</Spec>
<Spec label="Spatial resolution">

3 kilometer gridscale resolution

</Spec>
<Spec label="Data products">

Ensemble of 4 bias-adjusted climate model simulations, dynamically downscaled using the WRF model for the state of California: `EC-Earth3-r1i1p1f1`, `MPI-ESM1-2-HR-r3i1p1f1`, `TaiESM1-r1i1p1f1`, `MIROC6-r1i1p1f1`.

</Spec>
</Specs>

<Step n={1} totalSteps={5} title="Data retrieval and preprocessing">

Using Cal-Adapt's open source Python library `climakitae`, input data was retrieved. Climate model simulations were retrieved for the modeled historical period (1981-2014) and the future period under the SSP3-7.0 scenario (2015-2100). Inland water was masked from the data to discard artificially high values over water that are not representative of the variable.

</Step>

<Step n={2} totalSteps={5} title="Compute CDD/HDD values">

Cooling degree days (CDDs) are calculated by subtracting the cooling threshold value (65 °F) from each day's average temperature, producing a measurement of how many degrees each day's temperature exceeds the threshold by. Days with an average temperature below the threshold are counted as zero.

Heating degree days are calculated in a similar way by measuring how many degrees each day's average temperature falls below the heating threshold (65 °F). Days with an average temperature above the threshold are counted as zero.

<Callout kind="example" label="Plain language example">

A day with an average daily temperature of 75 °F has a CDD value of (75 °F - 65 °F) = 10. This day is above the heating threshold, so it has an HDD value of 0.

A day with an average daily temperature of 50 °F has an HDD value of (65 °F - 50 °F) = 15. This day is below the cooling threshold, so it has a CDD value of 0.

</Callout>

<Callout kind="note">

HDD and CDD are computed over a full calendar year, so they are not computed for years with incomplete data. The model data runs from September 1980 to August 2100. As a result, values for this metric are available for 1981–2099, even though the model data partially covers 1980 and 2100.

</Callout>

</Step>

<Step n={3} totalSteps={5} title="Annual and seasonal sums">

The daily values are summed annually to produce annual CDDs/HDDs, and seasonally to produce seasonal CDDs/HDDs.

</Step>

<Step n={4} totalSteps={5} title="Spatial aggregation">

The gridded data is aggregated by spatial unit (e.g. county, city, etc) by taking a **median** of all the grid cells within the boundary. A median is used to capture the typical value of CDD/HDD within the area of interest without introducing bias from any anomalous grid cells.

</Step>

<Step n={5} totalSteps={5} title="Model mean and range">

For each year of spatially aggregated data, the mean, minimum, and maximum values are taken across the four dynamically downscaled WRF simulations. The multi-model mean is used to plot the timeseries for CDD and HDD, with a shaded area indicating the range between the minimum and maximum model values.

</Step>
